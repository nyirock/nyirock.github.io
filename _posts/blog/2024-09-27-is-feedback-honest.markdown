Is feedback honest?
I have never thought much of peer feedback as it commonly understood, both, in terms of providing and receiving it. I'm sorry to say this, but reading the relevant book chapter did not change my mind by a lot. Does it mean that I disregard the feedback of my peers or am reluctant to provide my opinion about someone's work? Nothing could not be farther from the truth.
I applaud the example the author used describing his "furtive feedback" approach. While I have never watched anyone reading my work quite that way, this type of feedback seems effective. Partly because I would prefer the non-verbal cues myself as those tend to be immediate, more honest, true responses unobstructed by social constraints. Of course, one might find a number of friends, colleagues or a relatives who could convey their honest and true feedback verbally. Such feedback is rare and the writers are very lucky. The same works the other way - when feedback is provided. Very few people can handle honesty well, so it has become the norm to mollify our responses so that we stay nice, civil, and respectful. Which, of course, detracts from the main purpose of the concept.
