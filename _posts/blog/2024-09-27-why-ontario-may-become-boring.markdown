The moment I had read a book that the best kind of thinking goes from little experiences to implication of grandiose proportions - it really hit the nail, as I got such incidents in plenty. In fact, there's one such thing that recently keeps me awake at night. That is, Ontario, a province in Canada (to avoid ambiguity), might be on its downward trajectory of becoming a very gloomy and boring place indeed. It might sound far-fetched at first, but, please, hear me out.

My first suspicions crept in after meeting an uncommonly large number of unsuspecting Ontarians on a continual basis at the most unexpected of places in my daily life. For example, it's a well-known fact that a random non-Albertan person you meet has an above average chance to originate from Ontario. So far, I have tested this at work, in my French class, and at the grocery store next to me. Also, my flatmate is from Ontario. I suggest the reader might try it out in other places as well: fuel stations, public toilets, gym lockers, coffee line-ups, what have you.

Now, to a more sinister turn. Every single one of those unsuspecting Ontarians has, in fact, been quite an interesting person: smart, personable, with a sense of humor, an out-of-the box thinker. Of course, having lived in Ontario has caused them to retain some degree of emotional scarring, but after a beer or two they all seemed alright.

Finally to the issue at hand. While I'm wholeheartedly sympathetic to the cause of caring for the stray Ontarians, there is a bigger problem underway - what happens to Ontario itself? Is it being gradually depleted of its brightest and best? If so, the consequences of such ostracism are simply too hard to imagine. Depression, overeating, anxiety, driving fatigue, and losing at hokey are already on a rise in this province. How the ramifications of such migration will affect the various demographics there, and what unprotected social categories would be impacted the most in the long run? These all are the valid questions that need to be addressed. And they'd better be; before it is too late. In the meantime, I'll keep a watchful stance and will report selflessly any significant changes on the situation. But remember, we need to stand strong and united in the times like this.
